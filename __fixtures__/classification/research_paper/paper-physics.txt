Observation of Anomalous Hall Effect in a Twisted Bilayer Graphene Moiré Superlattice at Half Filling

Abstract
We report transport measurements on a twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) device with a twist angle of 1.08° near the first magic angle. At half filling of the flat moiré bands (ν = ±2), we observe a quantized anomalous Hall effect (QAHE) with a Hall resistance of h/e² ≈ 25.8 kΩ and vanishing longitudinal resistance below 100 mK, in the absence of any external magnetic field. This Chern insulating state emerges spontaneously from a correlated insulating phase upon application of a small perpendicular displacement field (D ≈ 0.3 V/nm). By mapping the phase diagram as a function of carrier density and displacement field, we identify four distinct Chern number transitions (C = 0 → ±1 → ±2) consistent with a valley-polarized orbital ferromagnet. Our results provide direct experimental evidence for the theoretical prediction that interaction-driven symmetry breaking in TBG can produce topologically non-trivial ground states.

1. Introduction
Twisted bilayer graphene at near-magic angles hosts remarkably flat electronic bands with bandwidths on the order of 10 meV, leading to strongly correlated electron physics at partial band fillings. The discovery of superconductivity (Cao et al., 2018) and correlated insulating states at integer fillings (ν = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3) sparked intense theoretical and experimental interest. Theoretical work by Bultinck et al. (2020) and Song et al. (2019) predicted that at half filling (ν = ±2), the combination of strong electron correlations and the non-trivial topology of the flat bands could stabilize a Chern insulator with spontaneous time-reversal symmetry breaking.

2. Device Fabrication and Measurement
Our TBG device was fabricated using the standard tear-and-stack technique. A monolayer graphene flake was exfoliated onto a polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) stamp, torn in half, and the two halves were stacked with a controlled twist angle, verified by Raman spectroscopy and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) to be 1.08° ± 0.02°. The heterostructure was encapsulated between two hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) layers and assembled on a graphite back gate. Edge contacts were fabricated using one-dimensional edge contact geometry (Wang et al., 2013) to minimize contact resistance. Measurements were performed in a dilution refrigerator with a base temperature of 15 mK, equipped with a superconducting vector magnet.

3. Results
3.1 Correlated Insulating States. At zero displacement field, we observe insulating states at all integer fillings ν = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3, consistent with prior reports. The insulating gap at ν = ±2 is approximately 0.4 meV.

3.2 Anomalous Hall Effect. Upon applying a perpendicular displacement field D, the ν = ±2 insulating state undergoes a transition to a Chern insulating state. At D = 0.31 V/nm and ν = -2, we measure a Hall resistance Rxy = 25.78 kΩ, within 0.2% of the quantized value h/e². Simultaneously, the longitudinal resistance drops below our measurement noise floor of 50 Ω, indicating dissipationless edge transport.

3.3 Chern Number Transitions. By sweeping the displacement field from -0.5 to +0.5 V/nm at fixed ν = -2, we observe a sequence of Hall plateaus at Rxy = 0, ±h/(2e²), and ±h/e², corresponding to Chern numbers C = 0, ±1, and ±2. The transitions between plateaus are hysteretic, suggesting first-order phase transitions.

4. Discussion
The observation of QAHE in TBG confirms the theoretical prediction that the interplay of flat-band topology and electron correlations can produce topological order without Landau levels. The sequence of Chern number transitions is consistent with a simple model in which the displacement field tunes the balance between sublattice polarization and valley polarization.

5. Conclusion
We have demonstrated the quantized anomalous Hall effect in twisted bilayer graphene at half filling, providing a tunable platform for studying topological matter in a purely two-dimensional carbon system.
