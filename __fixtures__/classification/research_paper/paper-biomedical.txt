Phase II Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial of Nilotinib in Early Parkinson's Disease: Effects on Motor Symptom Progression and Dopaminergic Imaging Biomarkers

Abstract
Background: Preclinical evidence suggests that the tyrosine kinase inhibitor nilotinib may promote autophagic clearance of alpha-synuclein aggregates and enhance dopamine transporter function. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of nilotinib in patients with early Parkinson's disease (PD) in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II trial.

Methods: We randomized 150 patients with idiopathic PD (Hoehn & Yahr Stage 1-2, disease duration <3 years) in a 2:1 ratio to receive nilotinib 150mg daily (n=99) or matching placebo (n=51) for 12 months. The primary endpoint was change in Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Part III (motor examination) score from baseline to month 12. Key secondary endpoints included dopamine transporter (DaT) binding measured by 123I-Ioflupane SPECT imaging, total UPDRS score, and Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39) quality of life score.

Results: At month 12, the nilotinib group showed a mean improvement in UPDRS Part III score of -2.8 points (95% CI: -4.1 to -1.5), compared with a worsening of +1.2 points (95% CI: -0.3 to +2.7) in the placebo group (between-group difference: -4.0 points; p=0.003). DaT-SPECT binding in the putamen declined by 8.2% in the placebo group but only 2.1% in the nilotinib group (p=0.018). PDQ-39 scores improved by 4.7 points in the nilotinib group versus 1.1 points in the placebo group (p=0.041). Adverse events were generally mild; the most common were nausea (nilotinib 18% vs. placebo 8%), peripheral edema (12% vs. 4%), and elevated liver enzymes (9% vs. 4%). Three patients in the nilotinib group discontinued due to QTc prolongation >500ms, all of which resolved upon discontinuation.

Conclusions: Low-dose nilotinib demonstrated statistically significant and clinically meaningful slowing of motor symptom progression in early PD over 12 months, with corresponding biomarker evidence of dopaminergic neuroprotection. These results support advancement to a definitive Phase III trial.

Keywords: Parkinson's disease, nilotinib, neuroprotection, alpha-synuclein, dopamine transporter, SPECT imaging

Introduction
Parkinson's disease is characterized by the progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and the accumulation of misfolded alpha-synuclein protein. Current treatments provide symptomatic relief but do not modify disease progression. The c-Abl tyrosine kinase pathway has been implicated in alpha-synuclein-mediated neurotoxicity (Hebron et al., 2013), and nilotinib, a brain-penetrant c-Abl inhibitor approved for chronic myeloid leukemia, has shown neuroprotective effects in animal models of PD (Pagan et al., 2016).

Methods
Study Design: This was a Phase II, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted at 24 academic movement disorders centers in the United States, Europe, and Israel (NCT04572216). The protocol was approved by institutional review boards at all participating sites.

Participants: Eligible patients were aged 40-75 years with a diagnosis of idiopathic PD per UK Brain Bank criteria, Hoehn & Yahr Stage 1 or 2, disease duration of less than 3 years from motor symptom onset, and were on a stable dose of levodopa and/or dopamine agonist for at least 30 days prior to screening.

Randomization and Blinding: Patients were randomized 2:1 using a centralized interactive web response system, stratified by study site and baseline UPDRS Part III score (<20 vs. >=20). All study personnel, patients, and outcome assessors remained blinded throughout the study.

Statistical Analysis: The primary analysis was performed on the modified intention-to-treat population (all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study medication and had at least one post-baseline UPDRS assessment). The primary endpoint was analyzed using a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) with fixed effects for treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline score, and stratification factors, and an unstructured covariance matrix.

Results
Of 150 randomized patients, 144 (96%) completed the 12-month study. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between groups. The mean age was 58.4 years, 62% were male, and the mean baseline UPDRS Part III score was 22.7.

Discussion
This trial provides the strongest evidence to date that targeting the c-Abl pathway may slow disease progression in early PD. The 4.0-point between-group difference on UPDRS Part III exceeds the minimum clinically important difference of 3.5 points established by movement disorder specialists (Schrag et al., 2006). The DaT-SPECT findings provide objective biomarker support for a disease-modifying effect rather than a purely symptomatic one.
