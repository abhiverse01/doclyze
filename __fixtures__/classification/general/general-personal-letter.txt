Dear Margaret,

I hope this letter finds you well. I've been meaning to write for weeks, but between the new job and the move, life has been a whirlwind since January.

First of all: I'm sorry I missed your retirement party. I had every intention of coming, but the moving truck arrived that weekend (of course), and I couldn't reschedule. Please know that I was thinking of you. Thirty-two years at Lincoln Elementary is remarkable, and I know how much you meant to those kids. I still remember when you taught Sarah—she still talks about your read-aloud sessions. You gave her a love of stories that I could never have instilled on my own.

As for me, the new position at Cambridge is going well, though it's a big change. I'm Vice President of Clinical Development now, which sounds impressive until you realize it mostly means sitting in very long meetings and writing very detailed emails. But the science is exciting. We're working on a Parkinson's drug that actually modifies disease progression rather than just treating symptoms. I can't share details yet—FDA and all that—but I feel hopeful in a way I haven't felt about a project since graduate school.

The house in Brookline is lovely but smaller than our place in Lexington. The kids seem to have adapted better than I expected. Emma has already made friends on the block (she has a talent for that), and Ben is mostly just excited about being closer to Fenway. We're still unpacking. How is it possible to accumulate so many books? David says we should just get a Kindle, which tells you everything you need to know about David.

I heard from your daughter that you're planning a trip to Portugal in May. If you haven't booked your hotel in Porto yet, we have a recommendation—a small guesthouse in Ribeira with the most incredible view of the Douro. I'll text you the name.

Let's plan to get together this spring. Maybe that Portuguese place in Inman Square that you like? My treat, to make up for missing the party.

Give my best to Richard.

With love and gratitude,
Amanda
